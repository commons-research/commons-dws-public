Open Science Is A Research Accelerator

Open Science is a Research Accelerator

Terms

  • Open Science: conducting research transparently, sharing data, methods, and results as work progresses.
  • Open-source research: applying open-source software development principles to scientific research.
  • Electronic Lab Notebook (ELN): online notebook where experiments, data, and observations are recorded and shared.
  • Kernel of activity: initial funded work that gives others something concrete to contribute to.
  • Crowdsourcing: solving problems by drawing contributions from a large community.
  • Post-publication peer review: scientific review that continues after publication through community discussion.

Intro

They argue that openness is not simply about making research freely available; it actively accelerates scientific discovery by allowing the right experts to discover and contribute to a project.

Open Source Research

They compare traditional research with open-source software development. Traditional ("cathedral"):

  • closed teams
  • hierarchical
  • expertise confined to known collaborators
  • results published near the end Open ("bazaar"):
  • low barrier to participation
  • anyone with relevant expertise can contribute
  • iterative improvements
  • work is shared continuously
  • experts self-select into the project

Case Study

They demonstrate the approach through the development of an improved synthesis pathway for the drug praziquantel (PZQ). Rather than keeping experiments private:

  • all experimental data were posted publicly
  • experiments recorded in an open electronic lab notebook
  • the community suggested new approaches
  • industry experts volunteered specialized knowledge
  • the research direction changed based on community feedback

The project reached a successful synthesis through contributions from people the researchers had never previously met.

Why Open Science Worked

The acceleration did not happen because thousands of people participated. Instead:

  • the right experts found the project
  • everyone could immediately build on previous work
  • failures were shared alongside successes
  • ideas were tested in parallel instead of sequentially
  • no time was lost waiting for formal publication

Successful Open Projects Need

Open projects require more than simply publishing data. Requirements:

  • an initial "kernel" of useful work
  • low barriers to contribution
  • clear project summaries
  • simple collaboration tools
  • active publicity
  • multiple communication channels (don't rely on one website)

Critique of Traditional Science

They identify several bottlenecks:

  • slow publication cycles
  • closed research groups
  • publication bias toward positive results
  • negative results rarely shared
  • peer review only before publication
  • incentives reward papers over useful datasets
  • valuable data often remain inaccessible

Advantages of Open Science

Benefits include:

  • faster scientific progress
  • transparent research process
  • continuous peer review
  • easier collaboration across institutions
  • long-term preservation of project knowledge
  • greater leverage from funding
  • easier reuse of data and methods y.
