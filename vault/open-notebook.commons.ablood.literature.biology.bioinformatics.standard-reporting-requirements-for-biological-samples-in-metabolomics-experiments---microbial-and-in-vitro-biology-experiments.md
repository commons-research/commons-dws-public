# [Standard reporting requirements for biological samples in metabolomics experiments - microbial and in vitro biology experiments](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11306-007-0080-4)
Proposes minimal requirements for describing biological context of metabolomics studies. 

Overseeing organizations:
[http://www.metabolomicssociety.org/mstandards.html](http://www.metabolomicssociety.org/mstandards.html)
[http://www.msi-workgroups.sourceforge.net/](http://www.msi-workgroups.sourceforge.net/)

## Standards
1) **Ontology**: no major recommendations
2) **Microbial and in vitro biology metabolomics**: studies with any cell or organism that require a defined external medium to facilitate growth and propagation.
3) **Scope of this recommendation**: identify and develop best standards
4) **Aim**: not prescriptive (why not?), but create minimum reporting standards for describing methods in experimentation. 
5) **Proposed reporting standards on microbial and in vitro biology sample context: starting point:** 
	1) species/strains/bioreseource
	2) source of the strains and substrates
	3) experimental design
	4) inoculation procedure
	5) growth and/or treatment conditions
	6) time of sampling and of other events relevant to samples
6) **Proposed minimal set of reporting standards for microbial and in vitro biology metabolomics experiments**: Includes factors specific to metabolomic experiments. These factors need a more detailed description. factors for minimal reporting:
	1) *sampling* 
		1) what is time between sample removal from environment until metabolic activity truly stopped? 
		2) What was the temperature during process/
		3) how were samples harvested?
	2) *quenching*
		1) how was sample shut down?
		2) how is cell integrity under quenching conditions?
	3) *extracellular metabolites* 
		1) how were intracellular metabolites discriminated from extracellular metabolites? 
	4) *extraction of metabolites from cells* 
		1) how were metabolites extracted from cells?
		2) what is the estimated recovery? 
	5) *Normalization of metabolome data*
		1) how were the metabolome data normalized? 
			1) how and what step was biomass determined?
	6) *sample clean up / work up*
		1) how were samples cleaned up with respect to compounds that could interfere?
	7) *sample storage*
		1) how and how long were samples stored after colleciton, during workup and prior to analysis?
	8) *quality control steps*
		1) how was verified that no biotic or a-biotic changes occurred?
	9) *detection limit* 
		1) what is the detection limit of metabolites for the samples analyzed?
	10) *stability* 
		1) What is known about stability of metabolites during quenching, extraction, and sample preprocessing? 
7) **Best-practice set of reporting standards for microbial and in vitro biology metabolomics experiments**: Includes both metabolomics-specific factors from the minimal reporting standards and general biological factors that affect experimental outcomes.
	1) *experimental design*
		1) What is the biological question or goal of the experiment?
		2) How are samples, treatments/growth conditions, and sampling times related?
		3) How are extracts, repeats, and metabolome samples related?
		4) What phenotypic characteristics were measured?
		5) What other omics datasets were generated from the same samples?
		6) Preferably describe experimental design using a diagram or table.
	2) *biosource*
		1) What is the source/supplier of the cell line or strain?
		2) For natural isolates:
			1) Were cultures deposited in an international culture collection with accession number and relevant details?
			2) What taxonomic system was used to identify the organism?
		3) For mutant strains:
			1) What wild-type were they derived from and how?
		4) For higher eukaryotes:
			1) What is the cell type, organ derivation, differentiation grade, and subcellular location?
			2) Were cells immortalized or transformed?
		5) How were cells stored?
	3) *growth environment*
		1) What growth container was used (type, supplier, geometry)?
			1) What growth supports were used for adherent cells?
		2) What was the growth configuration (suspension, attached, batch, continuous, etc.)?
		3) What was the inoculation procedure?
			1) What subculturing or splitting protocols were used?
			2) What was the inoculation size or seeding density?
		4) What media and substrates were used?
			1) Type, supplier, concentration, and supplements.
		5) What environmental conditions were present (temperature, pH, gases, humidity, CO2, stirring, evaporation, pO2)?
			1) Which conditions were controlled and which could change during growth?
		6) What was the growth rate if maintained at a fixed rate?
	4) *treatment/incubation conditions*
		1) What treatment factors were applied?
			1) Biotic factors (competition, infection, etc.).
			2) Abiotic factors (physical stress, chemical substances, etc.).
		2) What was the treatment dose and vehicle?
		3) What were the pretreatment/treatment times and intervals?
		4) Were labeled substrates used?
			1) Which 13C compounds, enrichment, purity, and labeling protocols?
		5) What pretreatment was performed, if any?
	5) *harvesting*
		1) What were the biological characteristics at harvesting?
			1) Growth phase/stage, generations, sampling time, stabilization period, culture passages, and differentiation markers.
		2) What were the abiotic characteristics at harvesting?
			1) Cell density, nutrient depletion, treatment time.
		3) What phenotypic characteristics were relevant to the research question?
			1) Yield, productivity, color, form, etc.
		4) Include harvesting aspects from the minimal reporting standards.
	6) *biotic factors related to sample work up*
		1) Include relevant sample processing factors from the minimal reporting standards (sampling, quenching, extraction, etc.).
