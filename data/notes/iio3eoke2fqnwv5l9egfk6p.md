# PhD Question Resources and notes
## [Dissertation and Applied Doctoral Project Components (nu.edu)](https://resources.nu.edu/c.php?g=1446805&p=10913030)
Research questions (RQs) need to be aligned with the research method and purpose of the study. Usually a central thesis question is the goal. Sub questions should be limited and guide data collection and analysis. 

Qualitative and Quantitative research questions are very different. 
- **Quantitative:** must reflect testable relationships between variables
- **Qualitative:** indicate exploratory and open-ended questions
Quantitative RQs lead to a hypothesis and reflect the predictions made for the research. Qualitative RQs do not require a hypothesis. 
## Common research question mistakes (avoid)
1) avoid yes/no questions. 
2) avoid interview style questions (do you think..., etc)
3) Too many variables - limit the number of variables being measured, multivariate research is harder to draw a causal connection
4) Assumptions baked into question (how does the failure of x...)
5) Topic too broad
6) Difficult data collection - consideration for the data that will need to be collected must be taken into consideration
## Developing a hypothesis in quantitative studies
A hypothesis is a single, tentative guess. It guides the theory development and experimental planning. 
### Nature of hypothesis
1) can be tested - verifiable
2) not moral/ethical questions
3) not overly specific or overly general
4) prediction of consequences 
5) valuable to know even if proven false
## [The Role of Sub-Questions in Strengthening Main Research Questions](https://www.researchquestionsgenerator.com/blog/sub-questions-strengthen-main-research-questions)
Answering the primary research question directly can be challenging and therefore subquestions are used to break the main question into smaller parts.
### Understanding Main Research Questions and Sub-Questions
#### Defining the Main Research Question
The main question is the *core* of the study. The question is broader than the sub questions will be intentionally. Requiring investigation of multiple factors. Main questions are broad but flexible and narrow enough to guide research. 
#### The Function of Sub-Questions
Decompose the main question into narrow focused inquires. Which:
1) Simplifies compexity - isolates aspects of the main question
2) Guide methodology - Each sub question aligns with a specific research method (can they overlap?)
3) Ensures comprehensiveness - covers all dimensions of the main question
### Strategies for Developing Effective Sub-Questions
#### Aligning Sub-Questions with the Main Question
Each sub question must contribute to answering the main question. So they need to be aligned both in content and scope. 
#### Ensuring Focus and Feasibility 
Sub questions should not be overly broad or open-ended. 
#### Maintaining Logical Sequencing
Sub questions follow a logical order that mirrors the research process. Foundational questions are first, then analytical quesions. 
### Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them
#### Overly Broad or Vague Sub-Questions
Being broader is for the main question
#### Redundant or Overlapping Sub-Qustions
Sub-questions should all be distinct, and should not overlap as much as possible. 
#### Misalignment with the Main Question
Must relate to the primary objective **directly**.

### What criteria should a good research question meet? 
- specific - subject should be clear
- relevant - relevant to field of study and builds on existing knowledge
- feasible - must be able to complete in available time, with available resources and knowledge
- verifiable - must be verifiable through emperical research or data analysis
- original - needs ot contribute to and expand existing knowledge
- inpsiring - must be challening and inspiring
